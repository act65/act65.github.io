Introducing [Rain](https://github.com/act65/rain).

This post says what Rain is for and how its pieces fit together. Two companion posts go further: [Testing the Rain protocol]({{ site.baseurl }}/rain-experiments/) covers what we want from it and the experiments that check it, and [Reputation as a belief]({{ site.baseurl }}/reputation-as-belief/) derives its rules from a Bayesian argument.

### 1. An economy of strangers

Imagine people who have never met being able to lend to each other, insure each other and trade, with no bank between them and, in some places, [no internet either]({{ site.baseurl }}/reputation-for-rohingya/). Each of these needs one thing first: a reason to believe that a stranger will do what they said they would.

### 2. Four kinds of trust

"Trust" in a decentralised system is four separate problems.

*   **Trust in the machine.** Will the rules be applied as written? A blockchain provides this, and Rain relies on it.
*   **Trust in the actor.** Is this one person, and the same person as yesterday? Rain gives each user a non-transferable identity token, which makes an identity persistent. It does not prove that a person has only one identity; for that it is meant to be combined with a proof-of-personhood system.
*   **Trust in the application.** Does this contract do what it claims? Rain cannot check that for you. It keeps every contract's actions in the open so that others can.
*   **Trust in the human.** Even with all of the above, will this person *choose* to keep their promise? **This is the problem Rain is built for.**

### 3. The idea: record promises, and what became of them

A **promise** is a small record: who promises, to whom, what, and by when. It is *pending* until it is either *fulfilled* or, once its deadline has passed, *defaulted*.

Rain is a public log of promises, and a **reputation** that is earned by keeping them. Everything else is built on top by anyone who wants to, as a **script**: a contract that records promises and moves money on behalf of the people using it. A loan is a script. So is an insurance pool, or an escrow.

![The pieces of Rain and how they connect]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/rain/rain-pieces.svg)

*The pieces. Users give a script its powers. The script writes to the promise log and can hold a user's reputation as a bond. An oracle reads the log and credits reputation for kept promises. Every promise pays a fee, and the fees are shared out among everyone holding reputation.*

Four rules make this safe to open to anyone.

*   **A script has only the powers you gave it.** It can record a promise in your name only if you authorised it, move only the money you allowed it, and bond only as much of your reputation as you agreed. Nobody can be made a promisor, and so nobody can be made a defaulter, without consenting to the script that does it.
*   **Every promise pays a fee,** a percentage of the amount promised.
*   **Keeping a promise earns reputation in proportion to that fee,** at a fixed price per point. Reputation is therefore a record of fees paid on promises that were kept. A large promise counts for more than a small one, and a new identity starts with none.
*   **Reputation fades.** Every period each score shrinks by a fixed fraction. A promise kept three years ago says less about someone than one kept last month.

![Where fees go and how reputation is earned]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/rain/rain-fees-and-reputation.svg)

*Fees and reputation. All fees go into a fund. Each cycle a fixed fraction of the fund is paid out, shared in proportion to reputation. The fraction is the same as the rate at which reputation fades.*

### 4. A loan, start to finish

The reference script is a fixed-term loan.

![The life of a loan]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/rain/rain-loan-lifecycle.svg)

1.  The borrower asks for a loan and may **bond** some reputation: it is locked, and only the script can unlock it.
2.  The lender pays the principal. Only now is the borrower's promise to repay recorded, and the fee paid.
3.  If the borrower repays in time, the bond is returned and they earn reputation.
4.  If the deadline passes, anyone can declare the default. The bond is destroyed. The lender receives a **claim token** for the amount owed, which they can keep or sell. The borrower is marked **delinquent**: they cannot borrow, and promises they keep while delinquent earn nothing.
5.  To clear their name the borrower must get the claim token back, which in practice means paying whoever holds it, and burn it. The destroyed bond does not come back.

### 5. What reputation is for

It is tempting to read reputation as collateral: bond enough of it and the loan is safe. That is not what it is, and the [experiments]({{ site.baseurl }}/rain-experiments/) show why. A point of reputation is worth its share of future fees, which is deliberately *less* than the fees it cost to earn; if it were worth more, people would buy it by trading empty promises with each other. So a bond can only ever secure a small loan.

Reputation does two other jobs, and these are the ones that matter.

*   **It is a record that is costly to fake.** What someone has repaid, and how much, is evidence about what they will do next. Every entry in that record cost a fee.
*   **It is a standing that is costly to lose.** A borrower who has worked up to large loans, and expects to keep borrowing, gives that up by defaulting. So does anyone who holds a role or a service that requires good standing.

The fee income is there to make accumulated standing worth something even to someone who is about to leave, and in the experiments it is occasionally what tips the balance. Most of what a default costs is the second item.

### 6. What Rain does not do

Rain does not decide what a good deal is. A script may lend too much against too little, or set a deadline nobody could meet. The log will record it faithfully, and a careful user or their wallet can read the script before agreeing to it. Rain's part is to make sure the record is true, that nobody is bound by a script they did not choose, and that a record cannot be manufactured cheaply.

It also does not solve identity. Being shut out only hurts someone who cannot come back under a new name, and how much that matters is measured in the companion post.

### 7. Where next

*   **More primitives**, such as a promise that only takes effect if another is kept or broken.
*   **Governance** of the few parameters: the fee, the price of a point, the rate at which reputation fades.
*   **Offline use**: promises signed while disconnected and settled later, which is where [this started]({{ site.baseurl }}/reputation-for-rohingya/).
