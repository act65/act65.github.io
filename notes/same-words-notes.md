I've Seen It All (depression)
Prompt:
A lone figure sits on a London Underground train, empty carriage, fluorescent light buzzing. Opposite them, an identical figure in identical clothes slumps in the same posture — their reflection, but wrong: darker, heavier, more solid, eyes open. The window behind the reflection shows a tunnel with no end. Cinematic, painterly, muted blues and sickly yellows, texture of an oil painting left in the damp. 3:2.

Lean on Me (loneliness as friend)
Prompt:
A dark, faceless figure made of negative space — an absence shaped like a person — stands in a small, dim room. It extends one hand, open, gentle, toward a seated human whose face is turned away, shoulders hunched. The figure’s hand is the only thing catching light. Behind it, the room stretches into impossible, empty distance. Oil-painting style, deep blacks and a single warm amber light source, like a Rembrandt scene hollowed out. 4:5.

Yesterday (dementia)
Prompt:
An elderly person sits at a breakfast table set for two. Opposite them, their own younger self sits in the other chair, smiling, present and solid, while the elderly figure is fading at the edges — like a watercolour dissolving into the wallpaper. Between them, a cup of tea is full and steaming on the young side, empty and cold on the old. Soft morning light, but it doesn’t warm the room. Dreamlike, melancholy, with the texture of an old photograph half-erased. 3:2.

Common People (poverty)
Prompt:
A man stands in a fluorescent-lit supermarket aisle, holding an empty shopping basket. Around him, blurred shoppers laugh and reach for branded goods. He is rendered in sharp, gritty detail — worn coat, tired eyes — while everyone else is soft and luminous, as if they belong to a different world. The aisle stretches forward into darkness; behind him, the exit door is visible but impossibly far. Social-realist painting style, Sickert meets Hopper, supermarket lighting as cruel as an interrogation room. 2:3.

Every Breath You Take (surveillance)
Prompt:
A vast hall of server racks, lights blinking red like thousands of unblinking eyes. In the foreground, a human figure sits alone at a tiny kitchen table, drinking coffee, while a shadow — not attached to any body — looms on the wall behind them, watching. The shadow is larger than a person, with no discernible source. Cold blue light, digital and clinical, but the shadow is warm black, almost breathing. Photorealistic with a creeping surrealism, like a still from a film that shouldn’t exist. 16:9.

What a Wonderful World (climate grief)
Prompt:
An old man sits on a park bench, eyes closed, smiling faintly. Behind him, a memory-painting floats in the sky: vivid trees, red roses, children playing, a blue sky — but the painting is cracked, peeling, and smoke curls at its edges. The real world around the man is grey, muted, the grass brittle, the sky overcast. The contrast is subtle — you notice the grey second. The man’s expression is peaceful, because he can still see the painting even though it’s gone. Style: a blend of photorealistic figure and expressionist backdrop, grief made beautiful. 3:2.
